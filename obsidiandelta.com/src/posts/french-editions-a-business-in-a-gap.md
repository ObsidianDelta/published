If you sell teaching material in English, there is a French market in Canada that your file cannot reach, and the people in it say, in their own surveys, that material is what they are short of. A Canadian French edition of your product, built from the editable source, closes that distance. You check the content. French-speaking teachers who use it check the French. The first edition is free, and an interactive version in both languages is the step after. For you it is a second market for work you have already done. For us it is a done-for-you service, so we say below what it is and what we have not measured.

## A French kit, built from one conversation

Open [Systèmes de fichiers imbriqués](https://daxfoundation.org/metadax/showcases/stockage-imbrique/). It is in French from the first line. A college teacher of second-year networking told us the hardest idea in his virtualized-storage module, a file system that contains another one, and then his real obstacle: the documentation is excellent, in English, and some of his class do not read English. The page shows what came back from that one conversation: a complete kit in French, six interactive 3D scenes you step through layer by layer, and a simulation where you can fill a storage pool until it fails, which nobody would do on the classroom's real server. Commands stay in English, as learners will type them, and the terms follow the French interface of the software on screen.

Be clear about what it is. Systèmes de fichiers imbriqués was written in French from an interview; it is not the translation of anyone's existing product. We offer it as evidence that a kit can be built with care and be interactive in French. A French edition of your product is the other job, and it starts from your editable file: the PowerPoint, the Slides deck or the Canva design, never the PDF you export for sale. French usually runs longer than English, so text stops fitting its boxes. A word search or an alphabet strip built from English words does not survive translation and has to be remade.

The interactive step is the kit behind [Words into Algebra](https://daxfoundation.org/metadax/showcases/words-into-algebra/): a page for the tutor, a page for the learner, five interactive 3D scenes. An interactive version of your product in both languages, built on that kit, is what comes after the first free edition.

## The gap, from three sources

Start with the size of the classrooms. Canadian Parents for French (CPF) compiles enrolment from provincial and territorial ministries of education. Its national table for 2021-2022, which leaves out Québec because the province does not collect the figure, counts [450,008 learners in French immersion and 1,306,512 in Core French](https://cpf.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/CPFNet_2025_EnrolementStats_v4_DIGITAL-2.pdf). Immersion teaches school subjects in French; Core French teaches French as a subject. CPF's current page says that in 2023-2024 [nearly 1.8 million young people](https://cpf.ca/fsl-enrolment-trends/) were learning French through one program or another.

Then ask what the teachers in those classrooms lack. The Ontario Public School Boards' Association surveyed first- and second-year French as a second language teachers in the province's English-language public boards, and 384 responded. In the report of the project's first year, [65% of respondents identified "access to suitable teaching resources" as their primary challenge](https://www.opsba.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/FSL-OLMP-Year-End-Report.pdf), on page 32.

For immersion, the Canadian Association of Immersion Professionals (ACPI) held a Canada-wide consultation in which some 900 professionals took part. Its final report, in the [Spring 2018 Immersion Journal](https://www.acpi.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Vol40_n1_Printemps_2018_final_en_web.pdf), says: "Teaching tools and resources specifically designed to support instruction of all subjects in a French immersion program are rare across Canada." In the vast majority of the provinces and territories it visited, the materials for teaching the curriculum "were usually designed and offered in English only," so it falls to the teachers to translate and adapt them. Seventy-one percent of the professionals consulted named the lack of resources and tools tailored to immersion as one of their biggest daily challenges.

Read these for what they are. The two teacher surveys date from about 2018, and one covers a single province. We did not find a newer national measure of the same question, and nothing here measures demand from your side of the counter: how many French editions would sell, we will learn from the first ones. The sources support the first half of the case: large classrooms, and teachers who say what they need is hard to find or arrives in English.

## What it means for you

The move this week: pick the one product teachers already ask you about, and find its editable file. Then send it. The first French edition is free. You send the file only if you want to, and nothing of yours is used without your consent. What comes back is Canadian French, with the typography and the grade and curriculum labels a French-speaking teacher expects, and the translation's copyright is yours. You check the content: the structure, the numbers, the answers. French-speaking teachers who use it check the French. If you sell through a marketplace, read its rules on machine-assisted work before you list the edition. They differ, the decision to list is yours, and we will tell you exactly how an edition was made.
