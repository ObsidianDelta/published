Electricity is not a product. It is drawn on everywhere, and it is dangerous to touch. Nobody who works with it does so bare-handed.

The same shift is under way with what most people call AI. It is becoming a general-purpose substrate: a new kind of electricity, drawn on everywhere rather than used in one place. And most of what is being sold on top of it is appliances — things you plug in and hand a job to. A chatbot. An assistant. An agent that goes off and does something in your name.

We think the useful question is not which appliance to buy. It is how a person works with the current itself, directly and safely, and directs everything plugged into it.

Our answer has a name: a **cognitive companion**. It is a new category, and it is not an appliance. It is the part of you that is wired in.

## The definition

We use the definition the [DAX Foundation publishes](https://daxfoundation.org/#cognitive-companion):

> An extension of a person — part of them — through which they work with nonbiological intelligence and direct it. It is not a separate agent acting in their place.

*Nonbiological intelligence* is the Foundation's term for what is usually called artificial intelligence. It names the substrate and nothing else, where *artificial* smuggles in a verdict that the thing is counterfeit.

Three things follow from the definition.

**It is part of you.** A companion is not a second party. You and your companion are one unit: its context, its trail and its capability are yours, and it has no interests of its own to negotiate with. In the Foundation's words, anything that acts in the person's place, or answers to someone else, is not a companion.

**It is how you direct agents.** Agents are going to do more and more of the work. Somebody has to tell them what to do, hold them to it, and check what they did. A companion is the part of you that does that — the hands, eyes and ears you work through when the work is being done by systems that are not you.

**It is not an assistant, and it is not an agent.** An assistant is a second party you hand tasks to. An agent acts in your place. A companion is neither. It is part of the one handing out the tasks.

## More intelligence, in a precise sense

The Foundation defines intelligence narrowly, as the navigation of constrained possibility space: the *how* of getting from A to B using what is known. On that definition a companion gives its user more intelligence. Not more facts, and not a second mind, but more reach across what is possible, inside the constraints that apply.

That is the whole claim, and it is deliberately modest.

## Name the thing being done

There is a naming habit in this too. Nobody watching someone play music on a phone calls them an electricity user; they say the person is listening to music. Calling everything "AI" is the same category error. It names the substrate instead of the activity, and it drags the conversation into good-versus-evil before anyone has said what is actually being done.

So on this blog we will try to name the thing being done. When we write about a companion, we mean part of a particular person: the part through which they work with this substrate and direct what runs on it. Not a chatbot, not an assistant, not a product category, and not a stand-in for the person.
