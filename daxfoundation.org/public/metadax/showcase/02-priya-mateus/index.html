Lesson 4: the “so what”

One focus only

This draft: reasoning. Claim and evidence are solid. The gap is explaining how the evidence proves the claim. Hold everything else.

The worked example to build from

Start here together, out loud first:

Claim

Our school should start a futsal team.

Evidence

40 students signed the petition in two days.

Weak reasoning (lesson 3)

“because it is good.”

Strong reasoning to work towards

“This shows there are already enough interested players for a full squad, so the team would not need extra recruiting to get started.”

Futsal is 5-a-side. 40 signatures is far more than a squad needs. That is the link.

Linking words to introduce

  • therefore
  • this shows that
  • because of this
  • as a result

Instead of red ink

After he writes his reasoning, read it back to him and ask: “What does that number actually prove?” Let him talk. Then say: “Put that into one sentence.” Save corrections for after he has his own words down.

When it’s tricky

If → he goes quiet after you return a draft

Put the pen down. Ask: “Talk me through what you were trying to say.” Transcribe his words. Then say: “That. Write that.”

If → his reasoning is still vague after the first try

Point to the evidence number. Ask: “What does 40 signatures in two days actually prove?” Wait. His answer is usually the reasoning.

If → he says he doesn’t know how to start

Hand him a card with the four linking words. Ask him to pick one and read the evidence sentence aloud with that word tacked on. The sentence often follows.

If → he adds new evidence instead of reasoning

That is a common move. Say: “Good, that is more evidence. Now show me how this evidence proves the claim.” Keep the focus on the original sentence.

If → you feel stuck mid-lesson

The Tell us & ask tab has a prompt ready for your own AI chat. Copy it, paste it, and ask. The session can wait two minutes.

Anchor point

His evidence is specific and strong. When he loses confidence, come back to this: “You already have a number. That is hard evidence. Now we just explain what it proves.”

Lesson 4: The So-What Step

Today’s one thing

Reasoning. You have the claim. You have the evidence. Now you show how the evidence proves the claim. That link is the “so what.”

What you already have

Claim: Our school should start a futsal team.

Evidence: 40 students signed the petition in two days.

Old reasoning: “because it is good.”

That last sentence does not show the link. It just says something is good. Compare it to this:

Strong reasoning: “This shows there are already enough interested players for a full squad, so the team would not need extra recruiting to get started.”

Futsal is 5-a-side. 40 signatures is way more than a squad needs. That is the link between your evidence and your claim.

Words that make the link

  • therefore
  • this shows that
  • because of this
  • as a result
